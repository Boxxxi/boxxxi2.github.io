01⌬RoboticsMIT CSAIL · NYU

THE BIG SIGNAL

MIT and NYU's FLEX turns one spoken correction into a robot's general rule

Tell a robot arm “don't spill on my laptop” and FLEX adds the laptop to the features its motion planner avoids, asks GPT-4o to generalize the corrections so far (here, keep liquids away from all electronics, so it also steers around a phone), then masks features no longer in the scene, in about five seconds. In simulated coffee, candle and bowling-ball tasks it inferred rules about electronics, flammables and fragile objects; the paper adds Franka tabletop tasks and a real-robot user study. It goes to CoRL this month.

MIT News ↗Nov 9Primary
WHY: Language corrections · robot motion planning · LLM generalization · CoRL
02✦AI ModelsAmazon

Amazon's HOPE prunes half a MoE's experts without breaking the teams they form

Expert-pruning methods usually score each expert alone. HOPE is a second-order method that solves a quadratic program over pairwise expert interactions, so co-activated groups survive. Across Qwen3.5-122B, Qwen3.5-35B and GLM-4.5-Air it had the best average rank of five methods; at 50% pruning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B scored 42.3 on SWE-Bench Pro against 41.8 unpruned, while REAP fell to 36.2. A calibrate–solve–prune package ships with the post; the numbers are Amazon's.

Amazon · Hugging Face ↗Nov 9Primary
WHY: Mixture of experts · model compression · second-order pruning · agentic coding
03⌬RoboticsAi2

PINNED

Ai2's ToyBlocks gives robot learning a controlled world where failures can be traced

The first of Ai2's “FoundationWorlds” is an open simulation of an arm and lettered blocks that auto-generates RL and motion-planned demonstrations in Isaac Lab, with no teleoperation, and lets researchers vary data and hold tasks out. Early findings: ManiFlow, π0.5 and MolmoAct 2 generalize poorly to new block positions and stack heights; a Claude Opus 5.5 agent solved 30 of 30 classic stacking plans but only 63% of search-and-spell tasks, mostly from letter confusions and wrong orientations. Code, data and models are public.

Ai2 · Hugging Face ↗Nov 6Primary
WHY: Robot learning testbed · VLA generalization · open simulation · agentic manipulation
04⌬RoboticsBoston Dynamics

Boston Dynamics rebuilt Stretch around uptime after 100,000 hours of unloading trucks

Stretch Gen 2 cuts 81% of the internal cabling, uses automotive-grade latching connectors and continuous-rotation joints, and moves to a gripper with a valve in every suction cup, so it can pick several boxes per arm motion at up to 1,000 cases an hour. Its battery lasts two shifts, a fast charger reaches 90% in about 75 minutes, and a full swerve-drive swap now takes an hour instead of a day. A good read on what field service teaches hardware design.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 6Brief
WHY: Mobile manipulation · design for serviceability · logistics robots · suction grasping
05⌬RoboticsMIT

MIT's UQ-DAAAM lets a robot spot and fix its own wrong memories

The system compares a vision-language model's captions of one object from different viewpoints to measure how unsure the robot is, picks more views, then has an LLM fuse them into a corrected memory, so a rooftop chimney first misread as a squirrel gets fixed. It resolves ambiguity for about 92% of objects with two extra captions each and lifts description accuracy 5% over the state of the art. The paper is headed to NeurIPS.

MIT News ↗Nov 5Primary
WHY: Robot memory · uncertainty quantification · vision-language models · NeurIPS
06✦AI ModelsOpenAI

OpenAI's agent pipeline produced 948 working Linux root exploits for $1.2M

In a mid-October run with an unreleased cyber model, the pipeline went from 2,308 candidate kernel findings to 1,724 reproduced vulnerabilities and 948 working local privilege-escalation exploits on a stock desktop distribution, at $1,279 per exploit in API credits. It also writes candidate patches. Human researchers review the output before reporting; the figures are OpenAI's own and the patches still await maintainer review.

WHY: AI security · autonomous vulnerability research · Linux kernel · cost per exploit
07⌬RoboticsAditya Mehrotra · MIT

BUILD CULTURE

MIT's combat robot club treats every destroyed machine as the curriculum

Student-run and open to any major, the club builds fighters such as ARES, a 30-pound vertical spinner with an aluminum, titanium and UHMWPE chassis, for the National Havoc Robot League. ARES lost twice at the 2025 world championships to weapons its armor couldn't withstand, was reworked, then placed second in April and third in October. A good template for learning CAD, FEA, CNC and welding by breaking things.

08✦AI ModelsAi2

Ai2's StateBench drops frontier models to 13% or lower on hard state tracking

The benchmark grades four algebraically defined levels, from immutable values to assignment by reference. Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Opus 5.5, and GPT-6 Sol and Luna handle the easy levels but fall to 13% or lower on the longest general finite problems. Trained directly on the task, Gated DeltaNet and hybrid models generalized almost perfectly on the first three levels out to tens of thousands of assignments, while transformers never learned the hardest one. Data, code and a report are public.

Ai2 · Hugging Face ↗Nov 3Primary
WHY: Benchmarks · long-context reasoning · Gated DeltaNet · architecture limits
09⌬RoboticsThe Robot Report

Tutor Intelligence raises $75M to turn paid robot shifts into training data

Andreessen Horowitz led the Series B, taking the Watertown, Mass., MIT CSAIL spinout to $117M. Customers pay by the hour or by the pick with no upfront purchase, while remote “tutors” help its fleets through real work and that data trains its models. It says twice as many robots now work in almost four times as many facilities as a year ago, and targets 1,000-plus under contract by the end of 2027; those numbers are the company's.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 2Brief
WHY: Robot deployment · teleoperated data · per-pick pricing · manipulation
010⌬RoboticsNVIDIA

PINNED

NVIDIA's Cosmos Framework adds runnable recipes for simulated camera and LiDAR worlds

The open framework now ships agent-runnable recipes and released Cosmos 3 Nano checkpoints that generate synchronized camera video and LiDAR for driving, robot and surgical scenarios, and it extends to GR00T, Alpamayo, pi0.5 and other compatible models. It's a practical guide: Nano generation needs 58 GiB in BF16 or 44 GiB in FP8 with offloading, and NVIDIA reports roughly 2–3× speedups from speculative decoding on H200.

NVIDIA ↗Nov 2Primary
WHY: World models · synchronized sensor simulation · open recipes · physical AI
