01⌬RoboticsAutonomous Robots Lab, NTNU

THE BIG SIGNAL

NTNU's quadrotor folds its own arms on contact to squeeze through a 15 cm gap

This week's IEEE Spectrum Video Friday leads with the Autonomous Robots Lab at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology. Rather than steering around openings narrower than the airframe, the team jointly optimized the quadrotor's geometry and the way its two side arms bend when they hit something, so the drone narrows itself passively: the prototype folds from 31.3 cm to 14.1 cm wide on contact and flies through a 15 cm hole in a plywood wall. The same roundup has TeCoBots, soft snake robots that link into chains, loops and branches with the claw they use to grab objects; AHEADance, a humanoid that predicts the next half second of streaming music and dances to it; a 3D-printed rigid-linkage hand with three actuated joints per finger for under $650 in parts; and CROBAT, a fully actuated flapping-wing quad-thopter that perches at a tilt.

02⌬RoboticsMedtronic

FDA clears Medtronic's modular Hugo robot for general surgery across the abdomen

Hugo, the cart-based surgical robot whose arms roll up separately so a team uses only the ones a case needs, gained FDA clearance for upper abdominal general surgery, including gallbladder removal, Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, ventral hernia and small-bowel resection, plus inguinal hernia and appendectomy below. Medtronic puts those at about 3.5 million US operations a year. It reached the clearance two ways at once: a US hernia study under an investigational device exemption that met its safety and effectiveness endpoints, and general-surgery data from Europe, where Hugo has had CE marking since 2022. Hugo first won US clearance for urology in December 2025 and is on track for 50,000 procedures worldwide; a gynecology study is running. Medtronic says AI from its Touch Surgery video platform will come to Hugo, without a date.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 20Brief
WHY: Surgical robotics · regulatory clearance · modular arm carts · clinical evidence
03⌬RoboticsMIT · courtesy of the researchers

MIT's flapping-wing microrobot now carries its own sensors and computer, and lands on a sunflower

Kevin Chen's Soft and Micro Robotics Lab, with Jonathan How's group and Cornell's Elizabeth Farrell Helbling, cut the motion-capture tether from their four-winged, paperclip-weight flier. The team made the soft artificial-muscle actuators 50% larger while raising net lift 46%, for a lift-to-weight ratio of 1.9, enough to carry a 244 mg package with an IMU, an optical-flow sensor, a time-of-flight height sensor and a microcontroller. A deliberately simplified estimator that updates position faster, rather than more accurately, kept drift low: hovering and point-to-point flights stayed within a few centimeters, and a joystick-guided run landed on a sunflower outside the lab. Power still comes over a wire. The paper is in Science Robotics.

MIT News ↗Nov 18Primary
WHY: Insect-scale aerial robots · onboard state estimation · soft actuators · Science Robotics
04⌬RoboticsRemedy Robotics

Remedy Robotics wants a stroke surgeon to steer a catheter from another city

A long IEEE Spectrum feature on Remedy's N1, a robot for thrombectomy, the clot-removal operation for ischemic stroke that only about 1 in 10 eligible patients receive in time. Instead of a guide wire, a robotic arm advances a steerable catheter whose tip is pulled by tiny wires; markings on the catheter let a computer-vision tracker correct for its twist, so 'right' on the X-ray means right, and a CT overlay shows the vessels X-ray can't. The system halts all motion when round-trip latency passes 150 ms. In September 2025 a Toronto neurosurgeon used it on ten consenting non-emergency patients, six from an adjoining room, two from elsewhere in the hospital and two from a hospital across town. No telesurgery system has full regulatory approval, and no US patient has been operated on remotely.

IEEE Spectrum ↗Nov 16Analysis
WHY: Surgical telerobotics · steerable catheters · latency limits · clinical evidence
05✦AI ModelsOpenAI

MedChemBench finds models can redraw a drug molecule but struggle to pick the next one to make

OpenAI's new benchmark turns 117 papers and patents into 99 medicinal-chemistry problems with 761 distinct structures. Each gives a model what a research team knew at one point in a program, such as the target, the goals, the compounds made so far and selected assay results, and asks it to reconstruct molecules from drawings, explain findings, rank compounds and predict properties like potency, permeability and metabolic stability, with partial credit for close numbers. GPT-6.2 Astra scored 84.5% at xhigh effort; GPT-5.6 Sol scored 58.2%. The split is the useful part: Astra earned 95.4% of the structure credit but only about half on selection and numeric prediction. OpenAI notes that the models may have seen the sources, that every answer was one attempt without tools, and that the benchmark says nothing about discovering new drugs.

06✦AI ModelsHugging Face

Inkling 2 Mini loops 12 shared layers three times to act like a 48-layer model on 3B active parameters

Thinking Machines Lab's small open multimodal model has 24 physical decoder layers but runs the middle 12 three times, so each token makes 48 layer visits from about 26B total and 3B active parameters. Every pass restarts from the output of layers 0–5, and in passes 2 and 3 each core layer reads gated messages from the previous pass into its query, key, value and MoE inputs; the router picks 8 of 128 experts per layer. Context reaches 1M tokens, five of every six layers use a 1,024-token sliding window, and experts ship in NVFP4 and MXFP8. Thinking Machines says it leads in 32 of 48 benchmarks against Gemma 4 31B, Qwen3.8-27B and others, and Hugging Face's own tests so far are vibe checks. A research checkpoint lets you choose 1, 2 or 3 passes to trade quality for speed. Day-zero support: Transformers, llama.cpp, MLX and SGLang.

Hugging Face · Thinking Machines Lab ↗Nov 20Primary
WHY: Local inference · looped transformers · mixture-of-experts · open weights
07✦AI ModelsGoogle DeepMind

Genie 4 lets you rewrite a generated world mid-session, with sound

DeepMind's newest world model reaches Google AI Ultra subscribers inside Project Genie. Genie 4 takes new text prompts while you walk through a generated world, so you can change the weather, drop in a character or redirect the action, and it adds real-time audio that follows the scene, from ambience to dialogue. Genie 3 stays in the model picker with upgrades of its own: up to 3 minutes per generation (from 60 seconds), linked worlds, and worlds seeded from 360-degree panoramas. A Gemini-driven agent builds worlds from a conversation and can trigger events while you explore. This is a product post with no benchmarks, but it is a usable look at how promptable, controllable world simulation now behaves.

08✦AI ModelsHugging Face

Streaming MoE experts from SSD runs a 180B model on a 32 GB Mac within 6% of Strata

Hugging Face's Tarek Ziadé made Transformers run Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (48 layers of 512 experts, 10 active, 121 GB in FP8) on a 32 GB M5 MacBook. The key trick is a slot pool: one preallocated device tensor shared by all layers, with router expert ids remapped to slots, so the stock grouped_mm kernel runs on a 12 GiB LRU cache as if it were a normal expert bank. Experts are quantized to 3 bits (49 GB on disk), eight I/O threads read misses, and the next two layers' routers run early to prefetch. Final numbers: 13.9 tok/s decode against Strata's 14.8, and 228 tok/s prefill against 220. The remaining gap is host round trips that PyTorch's MPS stream can't yet hide. The option is in an open Transformers PR, #50717.

Hugging Face ↗Nov 18Primary
WHY: Local inference · mixture-of-experts offloading · Apple silicon · open-source infrastructure
09⌬RoboticsAi2

PINNED

Ai2's ToyBlocks gives robot learning a controlled world where failures can be traced

The first of Ai2's “FoundationWorlds” is an open simulation of an arm and lettered blocks that auto-generates RL and motion-planned demonstrations in Isaac Lab, with no teleoperation, and lets researchers vary data and hold tasks out. Early findings: ManiFlow, π0.5 and MolmoAct 2 generalize poorly to new block positions and stack heights; a Claude Opus 5.5 agent solved 30 of 30 classic stacking plans but only 63% of search-and-spell tasks, mostly from letter confusions and wrong orientations. Code, data and models are public.

Ai2 · Hugging Face ↗Nov 6Primary
WHY: Robot learning testbed · VLA generalization · open simulation · agentic manipulation
010⌬RoboticsNVIDIA

PINNED

NVIDIA's Cosmos Data Factory lets a coding agent curate, augment and label physical-AI training data

A hands-on guide to five cloud-native blueprints and six composable agent skills (Auto Labeling, AnomalyGen, Augmentation, Curation and Retrieval, Simulation, Orchestration). Setup is one prompt to your coding agent: clone the orchestration skill and run its preflight, which checks the environment without changing it. A second prompt runs a blueprint: restyle driving scenes as rain, snow or fog while keeping layout and motion; vary clothing on person crops for re-identification; relight robot-manipulation footage; generate rare events such as spills, falls or collisions from a single image; or make defect images for inspection. In each, Cosmos Evaluator checks that the requested change actually appears and that motion is physically plausible, retries clips that fail, then auto-labels detections, tracks and captions. NVIDIA reports up to 60× faster dataset assembly on eight RTX PRO 6000 GPUs; that number is the company's.

WHY: Synthetic data for robots · world-model tooling · agentic pipelines · hands-on guide
