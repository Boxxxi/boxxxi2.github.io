01⌬RoboticsGoogle DeepMind · Apptronik

THE BIG SIGNAL

Gemini Robotics 2.5 puts an Apollo humanoid on Mercedes-Benz kitting lines at 96%

DeepMind's new vision-language-action model was tested on more than 200 tasks, from serving food with tongs to tying a bow and pipetting, with 30–100% higher success and 20–30% faster completion than earlier baselines. In a pilot at Mercedes plants in Kecskemét and Sindelfingen, Apptronik's Apollo 2, with a 22-DoF SharpaWave hand, sorted brackets from bulk bins into sequencing trays with a 96% success rate, using Gemini Robotics ER to track the workflow. Inference-time safety guidance raised safe completion near people by 43%, and a new ASIMOV-Jailbreak benchmark is open. The numbers are DeepMind's.

WHY: Vision-language-action models · humanoid deployment · dexterous manipulation · robot safety
02⌬RoboticsRunway

NYC ROBOTICS

NYC's Runway opens Praxis-1, a world action model already driving Standard Bots and Ultra robots

Praxis-1 is a 27B-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and actions together, built on Runway's video model; weights and inference and fine-tuning code (full and LoRA) are Apache 2.0, on GitHub and Hugging Face with LeRobot support. Launch partners Noble Machines, Standard Bots and Ultra run it on a legged humanoid, a six-axis arm and dual-arm packing stations. Runway reports 46.2% on RoboLab-120 over 1,200 episodes, against 36.8% for Cosmos3 Nano and 28.0% for π0.5, and 80.0% on SimplerEnv after fine-tuning on RT-1, against 52.4% for the original policy.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 11Brief
WHY: World action models · open weights · cross-embodiment policies · NYC startups
03⌬RoboticsUC Berkeley

Berkeley's GaP harness has AI agents write, simulate and fix readable robot programs

Ken Goldberg's lab and NVIDIA released open tools for “agentic robotics”: GaP turns a task description and CAD models into a behavior graph, then an execution graph that agents optimize by trial in Isaac simulation before an executor runs it on the robot, with sim and real feedback looping back. Student Eric Chen's RoboSimStudio puts the Newton physics engine's hundreds of parameters on sliders, and given a video, a frontier model used it to build a matching Newton simulation in about an hour. The VABAR suite benchmarks tool sorting, cable insertion and routing; Bosch is evaluating it and Ambi says it raised fleet throughput.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 11Brief
WHY: Agentic robot programming · simulation in the loop · industrial manipulation · open tools
04✦AI ModelsGoogle DeepMind

DeepMind's AlphaProof Code formally verifies Signal's protocol down to the compiled code

Humans and models wrote formal specifications of Signal's security goals, experts reviewed every one, then agents running on Gemini Carbon wrote more than six million lines of Lean proving that the compiled implementation, not just the protocol design, meets them, including protection before and after a temporary device compromise. Agents flagged about 1% of the proof for human review, and the job took four weeks. As a check, two real, previously fixed bugs were reintroduced into an isolated copy, and the system produced machine-checked proofs that each version was insecure. DeepMind calls it the first implementation-level verification of a widely deployed protocol.

Google DeepMind ↗Nov 10Primary
WHY: Formal verification · Lean · AI security · agent teams
05⌬RoboticsMIT CSAIL · NYU

MIT and NYU's FLEX turns one spoken correction into a robot's general rule

Tell a robot arm “don't spill on my laptop” and FLEX adds the laptop to the features its motion planner avoids, asks GPT-4o to generalize the corrections so far (here, keep liquids away from all electronics, so it also steers around a phone), then masks features no longer in the scene, in about five seconds. In simulated coffee, candle and bowling-ball tasks it inferred rules about electronics, flammables and fragile objects; the paper adds Franka tabletop tasks and a real-robot user study. It goes to CoRL this month.

MIT News ↗Nov 9Primary
WHY: Language corrections · robot motion planning · LLM generalization · CoRL
06✦AI ModelsAmazon

Amazon's HOPE prunes half a MoE's experts without breaking the teams they form

Expert-pruning methods usually score each expert alone. HOPE is a second-order method that solves a quadratic program over pairwise expert interactions, so co-activated groups survive. Across Qwen3.5-122B, Qwen3.5-35B and GLM-4.5-Air it had the best average rank of five methods; at 50% pruning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B scored 42.3 on SWE-Bench Pro against 41.8 unpruned, while REAP fell to 36.2. A calibrate–solve–prune package ships with the post; the numbers are Amazon's.

Amazon · Hugging Face ↗Nov 9Primary
WHY: Mixture of experts · model compression · second-order pruning · agentic coding
07⌬RoboticsNVIDIA

NVIDIA ships GR00T 2 and Isaac Lab 3.0, with Newton physics for cables and cloth

Announced at CoRL in Austin, GR00T 2 is NVIDIA's updated open world action model, trained on robot and human data including 100,000 hours of its open egocentric video; NVIDIA says it ranks in the top three on RoboCasa365, RoboLab, RoboDojo and MolmoSpaces. Isaac Lab 3.0 lets agents set up training environments, switches between PhysX and the open Newton engine to model deformables and many-jointed hands, and reports up to 2.5× the training throughput of version 2.3 on a dexterous benchmark. A new Video to Data workflow turns human video into simulated robot demonstrations, with an open challenge.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 9Brief
WHY: Robot foundation models · Isaac Lab · deformable simulation · open datasets
08⌬RoboticsAi2

PINNED

Ai2's ToyBlocks gives robot learning a controlled world where failures can be traced

The first of Ai2's “FoundationWorlds” is an open simulation of an arm and lettered blocks that auto-generates RL and motion-planned demonstrations in Isaac Lab, with no teleoperation, and lets researchers vary data and hold tasks out. Early findings: ManiFlow, π0.5 and MolmoAct 2 generalize poorly to new block positions and stack heights; a Claude Opus 5.5 agent solved 30 of 30 classic stacking plans but only 63% of search-and-spell tasks, mostly from letter confusions and wrong orientations. Code, data and models are public.

Ai2 · Hugging Face ↗Nov 6Primary
WHY: Robot learning testbed · VLA generalization · open simulation · agentic manipulation
09⌬RoboticsBoston Dynamics

Boston Dynamics rebuilt Stretch around uptime after 100,000 hours of unloading trucks

Stretch Gen 2 cuts 81% of the internal cabling, uses automotive-grade latching connectors and continuous-rotation joints, and moves to a gripper with a valve in every suction cup, so it can pick several boxes per arm motion at up to 1,000 cases an hour. Its battery lasts two shifts, a fast charger reaches 90% in about 75 minutes, and a full swerve-drive swap now takes an hour instead of a day. A good read on what field service teaches hardware design.

The Robot Report ↗Nov 6Brief
WHY: Mobile manipulation · design for serviceability · logistics robots · suction grasping
010⌬RoboticsNVIDIA

PINNED

NVIDIA's Cosmos Framework adds runnable recipes for simulated camera and LiDAR worlds

The open framework now ships agent-runnable recipes and released Cosmos 3 Nano checkpoints that generate synchronized camera video and LiDAR for driving, robot and surgical scenarios, and it extends to GR00T, Alpamayo, pi0.5 and other compatible models. It's a practical guide: Nano generation needs 58 GiB in BF16 or 44 GiB in FP8 with offloading, and NVIDIA reports roughly 2–3× speedups from speculative decoding on H200.

NVIDIA ↗Nov 2Primary
WHY: World models · synchronized sensor simulation · open recipes · physical AI
